# Measurement studio

THESIS: Make a quantum experiment legible at a glance, with reproducible comparisons as the signature interaction.

OWN-WORLD: A contemporary laboratory notebook translated into a working instrument: light green-neutral workspace, white plotting surfaces, fine rules, compact Manrope labels, tabular scientific values. Teal identifies the baseline, coral the variant, amber the separately scaled potential. Scientific curves are actual solver data.

STORY: Prepare a state, evolve it, inspect regions and moments, fork the exact checkpoint, change one potential, compare at the same time, and save the evidence.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Name and compare action, visible setup inspector on desktop, dominant position plot, real numerical measurements, momentum plot, and persistent transport. No landing-page hero or generated illustration. Mobile retains the plot and transport and moves setup into a sheet.

FORM: Code-led scientific canvas workbench. No raster comp is used as authority: the accepted audit layout and actual simulation data govern the structure. The user approved the proposed first release and delegated visual execution with “do it and make it pretty too.” No further design approval gate was introduced.

Grounded directions considered: calibration display, lecture blackboard, measurement notebook, modular instrument console, laboratory protocol sheet, scientific atlas, and experiment storyboard. The measurement notebook is the third grounded direction assigned by the design seed. Its clarity is raised by the console's consistent state vocabulary, the atlas's explicit scales, and the protocol sheet's reproducibility. Decorative strobing, handwritten controls, and organic map navigation do not serve this precision task.

MODE: Operate. Preserve author credit, scientific scope, custom states/potentials, drawing, eigenmode exploration, save/load, frame/video export. The first release includes branching, region probabilities, a momentum view, and a rolling density map. It does not promise a state composer, timed potentials, measurement ensembles, flux-derived asymptotic scattering coefficients, or GPU acceleration.
